    How this works
    1. Doves don't leave on a schedule — they go when a cold front gives them a north wind to ride.
    2. So we watch four stretches of country upstream of you, out to 620 miles north.
    3. Birds ride that wind while it holds — up to about 200 miles in a day — and sit down when it quits.
    4. The front outruns the birds. Cool air reaches you first; they land a day or two behind it. That gap is the whole point.

    Cold fronts we're tracking

    Each front is clocked as it crosses the bands, so its speed is measured rather than assumed. How sure comes from 31 separate weather model runs and how much they disagree.

    Are the birds actually moving?

    Doves counted per birdwatching stop across the whole flyway. Collared-doves are the check: they don't migrate, so their line should stay flat when the others jump. If all three move together, we're watching people, not birds.

    How much to trust this

    The weather half has been checked against eleven years of records. The bird half has never been checked at all.
