When the flight birds get here

A migration forecast for six fields outside Dallas. It watches four arcs of country to the north, finds cold fronts as they cross each one, measures how fast they're moving, and works out which morning the birds they launched will actually land on you.

Arrival forecast

Expected dove arrival at the fields, by morning. Each arc contributes its own pulse: birds leave when the front clears the latitude they're staged at, then fly south at their own pace.

Push index by arc

North at the top, the way the country lies. Darker means a stronger push out of that latitude band that day — wind, temperature drop, pressure rise, gated by season and by how many birds are left up there.

Fronts being tracked

Speed is measured, not assumed — a least-squares fit of passage time against latitude across the arcs a boundary actually swept.

Ground truth

Doves per observer-hour on complete eBird checklists in Dallas County — effort-normalized, so it counts birds and not birders. Eurasian Collared-Dove is the control: it doesn't migrate, so it should not track the others. If it does, we're measuring observers.

What's proven and what isn't

Eleven seasons of ERA5 reanalysis validated the weather half. The bird half is still open.

Validated

  • Front speeds, median across fronts, 2015–2025
  • Detector is not biased low — the live front's speed sits near the 15th percentile
  • ~7 full four-arc continental sweeps per season
  • Engine is deterministic across machines

Not yet validated

  • Bird ground speed — 150 mi/day is a guess, and it sets the peak date directly
  • How fast each arc's population depletes over a season
  • Pulse width, seasonal gate constants, all five component weights
  • Whether any of this predicts an actual bird
Forecasts are committed to git each morning before the outcome is known, so the record can't be quietly retuned into looking right.
